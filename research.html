Research

Research by Tedros Salih Abdu on 5G/6G terrestrial and non-terrestrial networks, TN–NTN mobility and handover, radio-resource management, satellite system planning, constellation optimization, and AI/ML for communication networks.

My research investigates how terrestrial and non-terrestrial communication networks can be designed, optimized, and operated more intelligently.

I work across multiple layers of the communication system—from mobility and radio-resource management to satellite-system planning and constellation design—using communication-system modeling, mathematical optimization, machine learning, algorithm development, and system-level simulation.

A recurring theme in my work is the interaction between three questions:

How should users connect? How should network resources be managed? How should the network itself be designed?

Research Areas

TN–NTN Mobility & Handover

How can seamless connectivity be maintained when users, networks, and satellite coverage are continuously changing?

The integration of terrestrial and non-terrestrial networks introduces mobility challenges that extend beyond conventional cellular handover. Satellite motion, evolving coverage, heterogeneous access networks, service requirements, and network availability all influence the mobility decision.

My research investigates mobility and handover mechanisms for integrated TN–NTN systems, ranging from demand-aware satellite handover to context-aware and AI/ML-assisted mobility management.

Current topics include:

  • TN–NTN mobility management
  • Satellite and beam handover
  • Context-aware and predictive handover
  • AI/ML-assisted mobility decisions
  • MNO–SNO cooperation
  • System-level mobility evaluation

Radio Resource Management & Optimization

How should limited communication resources adapt to changing traffic, interference, and network conditions?

A major theme throughout my research is the efficient management of communication-system resources.

I have worked on problems involving power, bandwidth, beams, traffic demand, interference, scheduling, and payload resources, particularly in multi-beam and flexible satellite communication systems.

My work combines mathematical optimization with heuristic, combinatorial, and learning-assisted approaches to develop resource-management strategies that respond to heterogeneous traffic demand and operational constraints.

Key topics include:

  • Power and bandwidth allocation
  • Demand-aware resource management
  • Interference-aware optimization
  • Scheduling and link adaptation
  • Beam and payload-resource management
  • Flexible satellite systems
  • Onboard processing-resource management

Beamforming, Precoding & Beam Management

How can spatial resources be used more effectively to improve capacity and manage interference?

My research background includes physical-layer and system-level techniques for improving spectral efficiency and managing interference in wireless and satellite communication systems.

This includes work on precoding, beamforming, beam management, interference mitigation, and multi-beam communication systems, particularly where physical-layer techniques interact with resource-allocation and system-level decisions.

I am especially interested in problems where beam design cannot be treated independently from traffic demand, resource availability, interference, and network geometry.

Beam Placement & Resource Allocation

Where should satellite beams be placed, and how should resources be assigned to them under non-uniform traffic demand?

My work also considers the joint design of beam placement and radio-resource allocation, particularly for large-scale MEO satellite systems.

These problems connect coverage decisions with the allocation of bandwidth, power, and satellite resources, while accounting for traffic demand, satellite visibility, payload limitations, and interference.

This research provides a bridge between dynamic resource management and the broader problem of satellite-network planning.

Satellite System Planning & Constellation Optimization

What satellite capabilities—and what constellation architecture—are required to deliver a target communication service?

My research increasingly addresses decisions that arise before a satellite network is deployed.

At the satellite level, I investigate how communication and service requirements can be translated into appropriate system resources and satellite capabilities.

At the network level, I study how constellation characteristics can be optimized according to coverage, communication, visibility, and service requirements.

Current topics include:

  • Satellite resource planning
  • System dimensioning
  • Constellation optimization
  • Orbital and coverage analysis
  • Satellite visibility
  • Communication-aware constellation design
  • 6G satellite connectivity
  • Direct-to-Device connectivity

AI/ML for Communication Networks

Where can learning provide a measurable advantage over model-based optimization and conventional network algorithms?

I investigate machine-learning approaches for communication-system problems involving mobility, handover, resource management, network planning, and sequential decision making.

My interest is not in applying AI/ML independently of the communication problem. Instead, I am interested in combining learning with physical models, system constraints, domain knowledge, and mathematical optimization.

This includes research directions such as:

  • Reinforcement learning for mobility and handover
  • Learning-assisted resource management
  • Prediction and context-aware network decisions
  • Hybrid optimization and learning
  • Learning-assisted system planning

Connecting the Research Areas

Although these topics can be studied independently, many of the most interesting problems emerge from their interaction.

For example:

Mobility ↔︎ Resource Management
A handover decision affects network load and resource availability, while available resources can influence whether a handover is beneficial.

Resource Management ↔︎ System Planning
Operational resource-allocation problems depend on the capabilities designed into the satellite and payload.

System Planning ↔︎ Constellation Design
Satellite capabilities influence the number and configuration of satellites required to provide a target service.

Constellation Design ↔︎ Mobility
Altitude, satellite density, visibility duration, and coverage overlap influence handover frequency and service continuity.

AI/ML ↔︎ Optimization
Learning can complement model-based approaches when decisions involve prediction, uncertainty, complex dynamics, or large design spaces.

These connections motivate a broader system-level perspective rather than treating mobility, resource management, and network design as completely separate problems.

Research Evolution

My research has progressively moved across different levels of satellite and wireless communication-system design.

Flexible Satellite Resource Management

My doctoral research at the University of Luxembourg focused on resource-management techniques for flexible broadband satellite communication systems.

The work investigated the efficient use of power, bandwidth, beams, and payload resources under heterogeneous traffic demand and interference conditions.

Research topics included:

  • Flexible resource optimization
  • Power and bandwidth allocation
  • Demand-aware resource management
  • Interference-aware resource management
  • Flexible payload-resource management
  • Onboard processing-resource management

This work established the resource-optimization foundation that continues to influence my current research.

5G Algorithm & System-Level Simulation

My industrial R&D experience at Nokia extended this perspective to terrestrial cellular systems.

As a Senior Algorithm and Simulation Engineer, I worked on algorithm development, performance evaluation, and system-level simulation for 5G systems, providing experience with 3GPP-based cellular-system development and industrial simulation methodology.

Integrated TN–NTN & Satellite Network Design

My current research extends these foundations toward broader questions involving TN–NTN mobility, intelligent handover, satellite-system planning, constellation optimization, Direct-to-Device connectivity, and AI/ML-assisted network decision making.

This progression connects operational questions—such as how resources should be allocated—with architectural questions about what resources should exist and how future terrestrial and non-terrestrial networks should be designed.

Research Methodology

My research combines several complementary approaches.

Communication-System Modeling
Link budgets, channel models, traffic modeling, scenario definition, orbital and coverage analysis, and system-level abstraction.

Mathematical Optimization
Continuous, discrete, combinatorial, heuristic, and metaheuristic approaches for communication-system design and resource management.

Machine Learning
Learning-assisted and reinforcement-learning approaches for mobility, resource management, prediction, and network decision making.

Algorithm Development
Translation of mathematical and system models into practical algorithms suitable for performance evaluation.

System-Level Simulation
Implementation, comparative evaluation, sensitivity analysis, and performance assessment under representative communication scenarios.
